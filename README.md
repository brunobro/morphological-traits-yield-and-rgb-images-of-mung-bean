# Dataset: Morphological traits, yield, and RGB images of Mung Bean (Vigna radiata L.) subjected to biofertilization

The mung bean (Vigna radiata L.) is a warm-season annual pulse crop widely cultivated across tropical and subtropical regions, playing a vital role in global food security, human nutrition, and sustainable agriculture (Dikr, 2023; Santos et al., 2025; Tang et al., 2014; Tarahi, 2024) . Containing roughly 20% to 32% high-digestibility protein rich in essential amino acids (particularly leucine and lysine), alongside dietary fiber, essential minerals, and bioactive phytochemicals such as flavonoids and phenolic acids, the mung bean serves as a critical nutritious food source, frequently termed “poor man’s meat” (Armin et al., 2016; Tang et al., 2014; Tarahi, 2024; Yao et al., 2013).

Furthermore, its seeds and sprouts demonstrate significant health-promoting biological activities, including antioxidant, antidiabetic, anti-inflammatory, antihypertensive, and antimicrobial properties (Tang et al., 2014; Tarahi, 2024). From an agricultural standpoint, mung bean features a short growth cycle (75–120 days), low water requirements, and the capacity for biological nitrogen fixation through rhizobial nodulation, which enhances soil fertility, breaks cereal monoculture fatigue, and supports sustainable crop rotations (Dikr, 2023; Ranawake et al., 2011; Santos et al., 2025).

However, global production faces major constraints from environmental stresses such as drought, temperature extremes, and nutrient deficits, alongside biotic challenges like pest infestations and viral diseases. Addressing these productivity gaps necessitates robust research into crop management, sustainable fertilization, and non-destructive phenotyping methods.

In this framework, the dataset presented here serves as a crucial asset for advance studies on mung beans. This dataset provides comprehensive quantitative evaluations of key agronomic parameters, including plant height, stem diameter, leaf weight, nodule weight, pod weight, grain yield, and relative chlorophyll content, harvested 60 days after planting across 18 experimental units. Uniquely, it couples these biometric records with high-resolution 24.2-megapixel RGB images (6000 × 4000 pixels) acquired under standardized artificial night lighting and a controlled dark backdrop.

By combining traditional destructive agronomic traits with standardized visual imagery, this dataset enables the development of computer vision algorithms, machine learning models, and non-invasive phenotyping tools to evaluate biofertilization effects and optimize crop yield in Vigna radiata L.
